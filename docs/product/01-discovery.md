# 1. Discovery: AI Act readiness for an AI use-case portfolio

Status: assumption-led, October 2026. No interviews were run for this portfolio version; the pilot
plan in [03-roadmap-and-pilot.md](03-roadmap-and-pilot.md) starts with them.

## The situation

A European insurer has a dozen AI use cases in production or pilot: underwriting scores, claims
triage, fraud signals, a customer assistant, a staff productivity assistant, vendor tools in HR and
marketing. The AI Act now applies in stages. Prohibitions and AI literacy have applied since
February 2025, transparency duties since August 2026, and high-risk requirements for Annex III uses
from December 2027 after the 2026 amendment. Life and health insurance pricing is named
in Annex III, so an insurer cannot treat the Act as someone else's problem.

What usually exists: a use-case register in a spreadsheet, a few legal memos, a training
catalogue, and a programme manager asked "are we ready?" by the executive committee. What usually
does not exist: one view joining the classification of each use case, the obligations that follow,
the evidence that each one is met, and the date by which it must be.

## Who it is for

| Persona | Job to be done | What hurts today |
|---|---|---|
| Chief data and AI officer (sponsor) | Tell the executive committee where the portfolio stands and what to fund | Status arrives as narrative; no one can say which systems are late |
| AI governance lead (primary user) | Keep the classification, obligations and evidence current for every use case | Rebuilds the picture by hand before each committee; legal memos and evidence live in different places |
| In-house counsel | Confirm or reject classifications, especially Art. 6(3) derogation claims | Owners self-classify; counsel sees the claim only when something goes wrong |
| Chief compliance and chief risk officers | Know where the organisation is already in breach and where it soon will be | Deadlines move (the 2026 amendment moved two of them); evidence is "in progress" for months |
| Chief people officer | Direct AI literacy effort where it matters most | Completion is reported as one percentage across the company |
| Use-case owners | Know what is asked of them and by when | Receive article numbers instead of tasks |

## Hypotheses (each with how it would be proved wrong)

| # | Hypothesis | Falsified if, in the pilot |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Preparing the committee's AI Act status takes the governance lead more than a day per cycle | Baseline in week 0 is under four hours |
| H2 | At least one use case in a real portfolio is classified differently once the screening is explicit (most likely a derogation claim or a forgotten transparency duty) | No classification changes after counsel reviews the first pack |
| H3 | Owners will answer a short structured screening if it is in plain language | Fewer than 80% of live use cases have complete screening by the second cycle |
| H4 | Showing overdue obligations first changes what the committee funds | No funding or stop decision in two cycles cites the pack |

## What is not known

- Whether counsel will accept a tool's proposal as a starting point or insist on writing every
  classification themselves. The design assumes the former and makes every reason visible.
- How the Commission's guidelines on high-risk classification (due 2027) will read the Art. 6(3)
  grounds. The rules file is versioned so the portfolio can be re-run when they land (US-15).
- Whether literacy completion data can be pulled by role group from the learning platform, or only
  as a company-wide figure.

## What this is not

Not legal advice, not a GRC system of record, not a conformity assessment. It is the evidence pack
a governance lead brings to the committee, built so that counsel can check every line.
